The Foundation

Enterprise Architecture (EA) is often misconstrued as a purely technical design function. We position EA as the strategic, socio-technical platform of competencies required to properly govern business-driven, value-oriented enterprise transformation. Our focus moves beyond mere systems design to the core objective of bridging organizational strategy with technical execution. By moving past objectives focused solely on cost consolidation and technical redundancy, we enable more informed strategic decisions and ensure technology investments are managed using consistent, repeatable processes across the entire enterprise. This elevated approach ensures that IT functions as a creator of business value, aligning with the executive expectation that technology must drive innovation.

Future Leadership and Governance Principles

Tolerating Disequilibrium

Organizational survival and prosperity are best developed through the intentional toleration of systemic tension and disorder, preventing rigidity.

Loose-Tight Control Systems

Implementing controls that focus on measuring outputs for which people are responsible, rather than controlling inputs, thus managing the tension between empowerment and control.

Complexity Perspective on Strategy

Shifting corporate strategy away from information processing and transaction cost economics toward models informed by complexity theory, recognizing the dynamics of complex adaptive systems within multi-business units.

Affordance Design

Focusing governance efforts on designing the organizational landscape to promote desired behaviors and discourage undesirable ones, ensuring alignment without strict hierarchy.

Cynefin Framework: Strategic Response Domains

Clear (Simple)

Domain where cause and effect are clear and linear. Management applies "best practices" and standard operating procedures.

Complicated

Domain characterized by multiple right answers. Requires expert analysis, investigation, and linear planning to establish "good practices".

Domain where cause and effect are only visible in hindsight. Requires the strategy of Probe-Sense-Respond, where solutions emerge through experimentation and feedback loops.
